No. You generally do not need your own ICANN registrar accreditation to sell domain registrations as a reseller.

A reseller must not claim to be ICANN accredited merely because it sells domains through an accredited registrar.

What Does ICANN Say About Domain Resellers?
ICANN describes resellers as businesses affiliated with or under contract with registrars that offer domain-registration services and related services to customers.

ICANN also makes the responsibility structure clear:

The reseller is not ICANN accredited merely because it is a reseller, while the accredited registrar remains responsible and accountable for registrar services provided through the reseller relationship.

See:

ICANN — The Domain Name Registration Process

and:

ICANN — FAQs for Registrants.

This distinction matters for both marketing and customer protection.

Can a Reseller Say "We Are ICANN Accredited"?
Not unless that reseller independently holds ICANN accreditation and is authorized to make that statement.

ICANN’s Registrar Accreditation Agreement requires accredited registrars to ensure that resellers do not:

display the ICANN logo improperly;
display the ICANN-Accredited Registrar logo as though the reseller itself were accredited;
otherwise represent themselves as ICANN accredited merely because of the reseller relationship.
See ICANN's Registrar Accreditation Agreement.

NiceNIC's current Domain Reseller Agreement applies the same principle.

A NiceNIC reseller can accurately say something such as:

Domain registration services powered by NiceNIC, an ICANN-accredited registrar.

But it should not say:

We are an ICANN-accredited registrar

unless the reseller itself actually has that accreditation.

Becoming an ICANN-Accredited Registrar Is a Separate Business Decision
ICANN accreditation involves much more than opening a reseller account.

ICANN’s current accreditation process includes requirements such as:

What Does ICANN Accreditation Cost?
ICANN’s current published financial considerations list:

US$3,500 non-refundable application fee
US$4,000 annual accreditation fee
variable registrar fees
other operational and registry-related costs
These amounts are only part of the cost.

See ICANN Registrar Accreditation Financial Considerations.

ICANN’s application instructions also require applicants to demonstrate appropriate operational, technical and financial capability.

Therefore:

Registrar accreditation is not simply a more expensive reseller tier.

It is a different operating model.

Registrar Data Escrow Is Another Example of the Difference
ICANN-accredited registrars have Registrar Data Escrow obligations.

ICANN’s current RDE program requires registrars to deposit specified registration data through an ICANN-designated or approved data escrow provider.

See ICANN Registrar Data Escrow Program.

A normal domain reseller does not independently become an ICANN registrar data-escrow operator merely because it sells domains.

The accredited registrar remains responsible for its applicable registrar-level obligations.

Does That Mean a Reseller Has No Compliance Responsibilities?
No.

This is one of the most important points in this article.

A reseller does not become an ICANN-accredited registrar.

But that does not mean:

the reseller can ignore domain policies.

ICANN requires registrar-reseller agreements to contain applicable obligations, and the sponsoring registrar must ensure the reseller relationship allows the registrar to comply with its accreditation requirements.

NiceNIC’s current reseller terms reflect this model.

What Responsibilities Does a Domain Reseller Still Have?
A serious reseller should understand at least the following areas.

1. Identify the Actual Registered Name Holder
If a customer registers the domain, the reseller should not simply put every domain in the reseller company’s own name.

The customer or intended legal entity should be accurately identified according to the applicable registration process.

NiceNIC's current Domain Name Registration Service Agreement specifically requires resellers and agents to handle Registered Name Holder information accurately.

2. Do Not Misrepresent Registrar Status
If NiceNIC is the sponsoring registrar:

NiceNIC is the registrar.

The reseller should not tell customers that the reseller itself is the accredited registrar unless that statement is independently true.

3. Make Required Registration Terms Available
Customers must be bound by the applicable registration terms and required notices.

NiceNIC’s reseller agreement provides for the NiceNIC registration agreement or another reseller agreement that incorporates required provisions.

The safest operational model is:

customer sees the required terms before the order is accepted.

4. Show Pricing Clearly
A reseller controls its own retail pricing model.

But customers should understand applicable:

registration price;
renewal price;
post-expiration costs;
restore/redemption charges;
additional mandatory costs.
Do not advertise:

$1 domain

and hide the long-term renewal cost until the following year.

NiceNIC publishes current registrar-side pricing through NiceNIC Domain Prices.

5. Maintain Accurate Customer Information
Registry and registrar systems can require accurate:

registrant name;
organization;
address;
email;
telephone;
eligibility information.
Some TLDs also require:

passport;
company document;
local presence;
other registry-specific data.
A reseller should collect the correct information instead of using a generic reseller identity for every customer.

6. Forward Required Communications
Some registrar, registry, security or legal communications must reach the actual registrant.

A reseller relationship should not become a wall that prevents required notices from reaching the customer.

NiceNIC may also need to communicate directly with a Registered Name Holder where registrar, registry, security or legal requirements apply.

7. Handle Customer Support Properly
The reseller normally manages the first-line commercial relationship with its own customers.

For example:

customer billing;
retail pricing;
storefront questions;
account access to reseller services.
NiceNIC handles registrar-side and registry-facing matters for NiceNIC-sponsored domains.

This division is particularly useful when a customer encounters:

registry rejection;
transfer exception;
TLD verification;
abuse/compliance case;
registrar-level domain status.
